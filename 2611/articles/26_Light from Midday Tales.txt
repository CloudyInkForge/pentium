Light from Midday Tales
DQ
My journey with English began in the quiet afternoons of my childhood, long before textbooks, exams and grammar exercises took over my language learning. When I was a little girl, my mum bought me a bilingual copy of Grimm’s Fairy Tales. It was not a strict study book, but a warm companion that shaped how I understand English even now.

Every day after I got home at noon, before my midday nap, I would read one fairy tale. I made a rule for myself: I would only read the English version and skip the Chinese translation. At first, the sentences felt strange and full of new words. I did not keep a dictionary nearby, nor did I stop to look up every unknown word. Instead, I tried to guess their meanings from the context, the storyline, and the feelings described in the text.

I kept this small habit day after day. Sometimes I guessed correctly, sometimes I only got a vague idea. Many words stayed unfamiliar, but I kept reading. I focused less on separate words and more on the overall flow of the story. Slowly, without realising it, I built a natural feel for English. I did not learn it through boring repetition. I picked it up little by little, lost in those fairy tales. Repeated sentence patterns and gentle story lines sank into my mind and formed my basic language sense.

As I grew older and had formal English classes, I realised how valuable that childhood habit was. Many students hold a common misunderstanding. They believe they can understand a passage only if they know every single word. They also think a text is impossible to understand if there are too many new words. But my early reading experience taught me something different.

English is not just a pile of separate words. It carries a continuous flow of meaning, connected by logic and emotion. You do not have to recognise every word to catch the main idea. Even with lots of unfamiliar vocabulary, you can still follow the story, work out the logical links and figure out what the writer means. On the other hand, knowing every word in a sentence does not always mean you truly get it, if you fail to feel how the meaning connects and flows.

This is the core rule of English. Language works through context, coherence and natural flow, not just word-for-word memorization. Those fairy tales taught me to stay calm when meeting difficult passages, not to panic at new words, and always look for the big picture first.

Up to now, when I deal with complex reading materials and prepare lessons, I still depend on that language sense I built years ago. That simple daily reading has become my greatest treasure. It reminds me that learning a language is not about being perfect in every small detail. It is about feeling the flow, learning to guess from context, and appreciating the beauty of connected ideas.

My students like to call me the Diary Queen, but I prefer the name Diligent Queen. Since I came to IVY, I have been really amazed by how hard-working the students here are. There is always a bright sparkle in their eyes when they dive into study. I strongly believe that deliberate practice will take us much further, side by side. They are already so excellent. With consistent small efforts in English reading, they are bound to shine even brighter. Dream big, do small. Let’s keep pushing forward together.

For me, English is far more than a school subject. It has witnessed my growth, a gentle power slowly gathered from those peaceful midday readings long ago.
